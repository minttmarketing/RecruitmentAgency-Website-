# Aceternity reference research

## Scope and method

Reviewed the four linked Aceternity template previews in a browser at 1440px and 390px, scrolling through the full page at each size and capturing viewport screenshots. The preview shell reduces the visible mobile site frame to about 360 CSS px inside the 390px browser viewport; mobile observations therefore describe the actual responsive layout within that embedded frame. Nodus, Simplistic, and Agenforce expose their live page inside a preview iframe. The live pages were inspected rather than treating the preview toolbar as part of the design.

The templates are references for visual techniques only. No reference copy, code, images, or exact page layouts are being reused.

## Schedule Marketing

- **Section order and purpose:** Compact navigation; centered scheduling promise and product mockup; partner logos; a short metric band; feature cards and product demonstrations; pricing; customer proof; FAQ; closing CTA and footer. The page moves from promise to evidence, then detail and conversion.
- **Navigation and hero:** A slim logo-and-links row, with the mobile menu condensed. The hero uses a short, large sans-serif heading, supporting text, a clear primary action, and a calendar interface as the main visual rather than a stock photo. Inter-like typography is clean and utilitarian.
- **Cards and layout:** Feature content is a mix of compact cards and larger interface illustrations, with even gutters, restrained borders, and medium rounded corners. Cards contain real product UI, not decorative icon-only tiles.
- **Background and motion:** Mostly quiet neutral surfaces with a warm orange accent, allowing the product visual to carry contrast. Any reveals and hover changes remain secondary; no strong cursor-follow effect stood out. The long page uses repeated content sections rather than one persistent scroll spectacle.
- **Spacing and premium cues:** The hero has generous breathing room; later feature and pricing sections become denser while preserving a consistent content width. Premium feel comes from a clear focal point, believable UI detail, calm surfaces, consistent card geometry, and a simple action hierarchy.

## Nodus Agent

- **Section order and purpose:** Centered product promise; trust row; integration capability; agent/workflow feature grid; industry coverage; workflow benefits and dashboard examples; customer proof; pricing; security section; FAQ; final CTA and footer.
- **Navigation and hero:** A compact dark header places the brand, a small set of links, and a high-contrast action on one line. The hero centers a large two-line sans-serif statement, short support copy, two actions, and a proof marker; the dashboard begins immediately below it. The mobile header collapses to a menu.
- **Cards and layout:** The feature area alternates large dashboard mockups with smaller workflow, integration, and status cards. Tiles use dark surfaces, thin low-contrast outlines, and moderate radii; dense UI is balanced by substantial empty space around each group.
- **Background and motion:** Near-black canvas with a restrained warm accent and bright interface surfaces. The dashboard is the primary visual effect; the page relies more on layered product mockups and section reveals than on a large animated background. Hover is visually quiet, with conventional links and buttons.
- **Spacing and premium cues:** A strong hero-to-dashboard transition, consistent alignment, and repetition of the same interface language make the long page coherent. Premium feel comes from focused headlines, plausible UI, intentional contrast, carefully grouped features, and a restrained use of color.

## Simplistic SaaS

- **Section order and purpose:** Large product promise; trust statement; a multi-card feature grid; customer proof; device and platform use cases; security/compliance; pricing; FAQ; closing CTA and footer.
- **Navigation and hero:** Logo-led header with product/pricing/resources links, account actions, and a mobile menu. The hero uses a very large sans-serif headline, a short explanation, and paired actions. The text is the focal point; product visuals and device illustrations carry the story farther down the page. Headings use a modern sans-serif system.
- **Cards and layout:** The feature section mixes differently sized dark tiles and UI illustrations, with a larger social-proof area and separate desktop/mobile device visuals. Cards use generous internal padding, rounded corners, and subtle borders against a dark canvas. At mobile width, multi-column areas stack into a tall single-column reading flow.
- **Background and motion:** Black and charcoal surfaces with a bright blue primary action. Product screens, testimonials, and platform illustrations supply variation; no persistent cursor effect is necessary to understand the page. Links, menu, pricing controls, and FAQ provide the main interaction points.
- **Spacing and premium cues:** The headline is oversized but surrounded by negative space; information density increases in the feature and testimonial sections, then eases again for pricing and the final CTA. Premium feel comes from clear hierarchy, consistent dark surfaces, varied but orderly card sizes, product-specific illustrations, and predictable CTA placement.

## Agenforce Marketing

- **Section order and purpose:** Brand/menu row; hero with product action; trust statement; a narrative feature overview; capability and interface cards; governance/security proof; pricing; FAQ; final CTA and footer.
- **Navigation and hero:** A compact logo row and menu button simplify navigation, especially at the mobile width. The hero pairs a two-line sans-serif headline and short explanation with a product action and a supporting UI visual. Manrope-style headings pair with Inter body text.
- **Cards and layout:** The page alternates larger feature statements with smaller interface cards for orchestration, integrations, approvals, and audit controls. Cards are dark, softly rounded, and organized by consistent spacing rather than strong ornamental borders.
- **Background and motion:** Dark neutral surfaces, subtle gradients, and restrained light accents keep focus on the product interfaces. Interactive controls are conventional; the page is built around clear content sections rather than an elaborate cursor or parallax effect. FAQ items provide a familiar disclosure interaction.
- **Spacing and premium cues:** The mobile page collapses its feature groupings into a linear sequence without losing the heading-to-example relationship. Premium feel comes from focused messaging, consistent typography, credible interface details, a disciplined palette, and a repeated rhythm of explanation followed by proof.

## Cross-reference observations

- **Section rhythm:** All four establish one product promise first, add trust or proof early, use feature/product visuals to explain the offer, then close with pricing or FAQ and a clear CTA. The exact section compositions differ; the common technique is alternating narrative and evidence.
- **Navigation and hero:** Headers are compact and keep a single obvious action visible. Mobile navigation condenses. Headlines are sans-serif and visually dominant, but their line lengths stay short.
- **Tile treatment:** Feature grids combine broad and narrow cards rather than repeating identical boxes. Cards use restrained borders and rounded corners, with UI, charts, lists, or device illustrations inside.
- **Atmosphere and interactions:** Dark palettes dominate three references; Schedule is more neutral and orange-accented. None needs a constant cursor effect to feel polished. Product visuals, intentional whitespace, restrained hover feedback, and strong contrast do more work than visual noise.
- **Scroll and spacing:** Pages are long, but section groups are visually distinct and the same container alignment returns throughout. Mobile turns the grids into a vertical sequence and prioritizes readable copy over decorative density.
- **Five recurring premium cues:** (1) one unmistakable focal point per section, (2) real-looking interface illustrations, (3) disciplined type hierarchy, (4) consistent container and spacing rules, and (5) accent color reserved for actions and meaningful status.

## Component licensing check

- The Aceternity Components index currently reports that it is coming soon.
- The [Bento Grid component page](https://ui.aceternity.com/components/bento-grid) provides a direct `shadcn` installation command and does not label the component Pro; it is the one confirmed free component planned for use.
- The [Blocks catalog](https://ui.aceternity.com/blocks) presents its section packs as All-Access content. No paid block or template will be copied or installed.
- Navbar, spotlight, scroll-reveal/timeline, moving-border, testimonial, beams, animated input, and similar effects will be rebuilt as original components with the project stack and accessible shadcn primitives. This avoids treating paid blocks as free components.

## What we borrow

- A strong first-screen message followed immediately by proof.
- Alternating narrative sections with visual evidence and varied-size feature cards.
- Consistent container alignment, generous section spacing, and mobile-first card stacking.
- Restrained hover feedback and clear action hierarchy.
- Small, purposeful interface illustrations in place of filler decoration.

## What we do differently

- Keep Northbridge Talent's white, mist, sky-blue, and navy palette; avoid the references' dark SaaS styling.
- Retain Newsreader for editorial headings and Instrument Sans for readable body copy. The references favor sans-serif pairs, but they do not offer a better fit for this light corporate, human-led identity.
- Make job search and employer hiring actions prominent throughout, not a software trial or pricing plan.
- Use a locally bundled recruitment photograph in the full-height hero, then explain staffing services, employer/candidate paths, featured roles, fees/process FAQs, and confidentiality.
- Label all invented hiring metrics and testimonials as demo content, and preserve accessible forms, keyboard operation, and reduced-motion behavior.
